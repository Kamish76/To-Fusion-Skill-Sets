# Prompt-to-Geometry Mapping Patterns

Translating natural language mechanical descriptions into explicit mathematical constraints, sketch curves, and solid features in Autodesk Fusion 360.

---

## 1. Spatial & Enclosure Descriptors

| User Prompt Phrase | Engineering Interpretation | Exact Fusion 360 Implementation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"No lids, just the thing itself"* | Open-top container / tray | Shell feature or extrude with top face omitted. Wall thickness typically `2.0 - 2.5 mm`. |
| *"Full height back & side walls"* | Asymmetric rim height | Extrude side and rear profiles up to master tray height $H$, while keeping front wall low ($0.4 - 0.5 \times H$) for easy part extraction. |
| *"In 2 pieces, connects somehow"* | Modular split tray with lap or tongue joint | Split total depth into `Front_Piece` and `Rear_Piece`. Provide `0.20 mm` per-side clearance on mating step tabs/tongues so they interlock cleanly without fasteners. |
| *"Cover front during transport"* | Invertible lid / transport seal | The rear piece has a perimeter rebate matching the front piece rim. When flipped 180° in the collapsed state, it nests over the front tray to prevent contents spilling. |
| *"1 row, 2 columns"* | Subdivided bin pocket | Longitudinal divider along center: width $W / 2$, divider wall thickness $1.6 - 2.0\text{ mm}$ with $R = 1.0\text{ mm}$ floor fillets. |
| *"Beside the whole assembly"* | Zero-clearance mating face | Check mating face centroids: $X_{\text{bar\_inner}} = X_{\text{tray\_wall\_outer}}$. Displacement must equal exact coordinate delta, not guessed offsets. |

## 3. The "Regular Hexagon Overhang Trap" & $45^\circ$-Crowned DFM Formulation

### The Problem
Pointy-topped hexagons are standard advice for avoiding flat ceilings in 3D printing. However, **regular hexagons have $120^\circ$ interior angles**, causing their roof edges to slope at only $30^\circ$ above horizontal (**$60^\circ$ overhang from vertical**). On thin vertical walls ($< 3.0\text{ mm}$), each $0.20\text{ mm}$ layer steps outward by $\approx 0.35\text{ mm}$ into thin air. Molten filament curls upwards at the apex, causing nozzle strikes, surface blobs, and layer delamination.

### The $45^\circ$-Crowned DFM Solution
Decouple the roof crown rise from regular hexagon math and enforce:
$$\text{crown} = W_h$$
Where $W_h$ is the cell half-width. This guarantees an exact **$45.0^\circ$ self-supporting overhang angle** ($\Delta Z = \Delta Y$), completely eliminating apex curling while preserving the honeycomb aesthetic.

Coordinates generated by `make_dfm_hex_points(cy, cz, w_h, h_v)` in `shared/cad_primitives.py`:
- Top Apex: $(c_y, c_z + h_v + W_h)$
- Top-Right: $(c_y + W_h, c_z + h_v)$
- Bottom-Right: $(c_y + W_h, c_z - h_v)$
- Bottom Apex: $(c_y, c_z - h_v - W_h)$
- Bottom-Left: $(c_y - W_h, c_z - h_v)$
- Top-Left: $(c_y - W_h, c_z + h_v)$

## 4. Closed-Form Isotropic Strut Thickness Formulation for Lattices

When arranging staggered hexagonal or diamond grids, setting horizontal spacing $\Delta Y = 2 W_h + w_{\text{strut}}$ while guessing vertical row pitch $\Delta Z$ creates non-uniform diagonal struts that either pinch shut or form fragile hairline slivers.

Enforce the exact closed-form relation in `generate_dfm_honeycomb_grid()`:
$$\Delta Z = \sqrt{2}\, w_{\text{strut}} + 2 h_v + W_h$$
$$\Delta Y = 2 W_h + w_{\text{strut}}$$

Where:
- $W_h$ = cell half-width
- $h_v$ = cell vertical side wall half-height
- $w_{\text{strut}}$ = target strut thickness (horizontal & diagonal)
- Result: Uniform wall thickness (e.g. $2.6\text{ mm}$) everywhere across the entire lattice.

## 5. FDM Display Box Proportions Rule of Thumb

For structural organizer trays and display boxes with wall height $< 70\text{ mm}$:
- **Avoid:** $\ge 4$ rows of micro-hexagons ($R \le 3.5\text{ mm}$, $w \le 1.5\text{ mm}$) — creates 50+ isolated thin pillars per layer that flex and cause excessive retractions.
- **Prefer:** **2 staggered rows** with cell width $9\text{--}12\text{ mm}$ and strut width $\ge 2.5\text{ mm}$ (~6–7 continuous perimeters with a $0.4\text{ mm}$ nozzle). This reduces retraction events by $>70\%$ and produces a durable, rigid lattice that prints cleanly without sagging.
